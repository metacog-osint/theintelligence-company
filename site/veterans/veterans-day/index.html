                  About this planner

                  How it works, limits, privacy, sources

                  What it is

                  A free planner for veterans and service members in Fargo-Moorhead on Veterans Day, Wednesday 11 November 2026. It builds your route from where you are, what you're after and the time you have left, checks you can get to each stop before its deal ends, and re-plans as you go. Optimal takes the best deal of each kind and never sends you to two of the same kind, such as two coffees, within 3 hours. Best for me weighs each deal against your time; Maximize goes for the most stops, the most savings or both. It learns from what you skip and collect, and veterans who use it share whether each deal was honoured, so everyone sees what's working.

                  Pick your area at the top: Fargo-Moorhead, Bismarck-Mandan and New Salem, or Plymouth, Maple Grove and Roseville in the Twin Cities. This phone remembers the pick, and a shared link can open an area directly.

                  Method

                  Offers come from each business's own 2026 announcements where we found them, and otherwise from 2026 deal lists (Military.com, Stars and Stripes and others) and local news. Every deal shows its sources and a grade: confirmed (the business said so for 2026), announced (2026 deal lists, often "participating locations") or not confirmed (only earlier years, or a single 2026 listing we couldn't confirm; call first). A few places with a deal in earlier years only are listed at the end of Deals, off the route. Deals that need a purchase stay off your route until you turn them on under What I'm after; bars are on, and turn off there too. A ticket for later (a Great Clips or Freddy's card) doesn't open your route unless it has to. Store locations are checked against the chains' store pages and OpenStreetMap. Outside Fargo-Moorhead, store lists come from the chains' own store finders, as collected by All the Places in late October and early November 2026, and from OpenStreetMap, whose stores are marked as from directories. Drive times are worked out ahead of time on the road network (OSRM, from OpenStreetMap) from a grid of points across the metro to every store, so your phone can time your trip without sending your location anywhere.

                  Limits

                  Chains let each store decide, and deals change on the day. A deal here is not a promise: when it matters, call ahead. Drive times are estimates for normal traffic. The app doesn't know about lines until someone reports one. Bismarck-Mandan, New Salem and the Twin Cities were added the day before Veterans Day, with fewer local deals checked than in Fargo-Moorhead and few 2026 times from veterans' posts.

                  Your privacy

                  No accounts, analytics or tracking. Your location, settings and history stay on your phone. A report you send (for example "worked at this store") carries only the store, the outcome and the time, with no name or ID. To stop the same phone sending floods of reports, the server keeps a scrambled form of your network address for a day, and then deletes it. The map's background tiles come from the OpenStreetMap Foundation, which, like any online map, sees which area you are looking at.

                  The route line on the map follows the roads between your stops. To draw it, the Map sends the stores on your route, in order, to OSRM's public routing server (run by FOSSGIS, a German OpenStreetMap association), which keeps requests in its server log. It never sends your location or your start point: the line from you to your first stop is drawn straight, on this phone. Your first stop is usually near you, so like the map tiles, the store list hints at your part of town.
