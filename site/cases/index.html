Noisefloor: GPS loss over California during a declared Navy test, September 2026

The published finding. On 22 Sep 2026 Noisefloor recorded two short bursts of aircraft reporting degraded GPS over the Bay Area and the Central Valley, at 18:00 UTC to 18:08 UTC (peak 13 aircraft) and 19:52 UTC to 20:12 UTC (peak 36), on a day of quiet space weather (Kp no higher than 1.7). It is one of the checks on Noisefloor's validation page ↗.

The source it was checked against. FAA flight advisory PMSRCA GPS 26-03, Revision 2 ↗ scheduled GPS interference testing on the Point Mugu Sea Range, centred at 34°21′28″N 121°41′32″W, off the coast of central California. GPS, including WAAS and ADS-B, "may not be available" within 567 NM at 10,000 ft and 623 NM at FL250. The test was scheduled for 15 to 17 Sep, daily from 18:30 to 22:29 UTC, and 22 to 24 Sep, daily from 18:30 to 20:29 UTC. The advisory says each event may last the whole period; it does not say that every scheduled day was used.

What this establishes

  • On five of the six scheduled days, aircraft over California reported degraded GPS inside the declared test hours. On each of those five days the largest event was bigger than anything in the same hours on the four days without a test (largest 17 aircraft).
  • The timing comes from aircraft broadcasts alone, collected by a volunteer receiver network, and was matched to a schedule that was published in advance.
  • The space weather was quiet, so a geomagnetic storm does not explain it.

What it does not establish

  • That the test caused any one burst. A schedule that lines up is consistent with the test; it does not show which transmitter was on, or when.
  • Why the burst at 18:00 UTC on 22 Sep began before the window opened, or why the days without a test are not empty. Both stay open.
  • Why 16 Sep shows nothing. The test may not have run that day; the advisory does not say.
  • How many aircraft were affected in all. The counts are a lower bound: they include only aircraft the receiver network heard, after Noisefloor's filters. An independent account counted about 40 and 80 aircraft above 8,000 ft in the two 22 Sep bursts.
  • The 22 Sep file was built with the earlier within-day burst threshold; the other days use the threshold from the same time slot on the previous 14 days, so counts are not exactly comparable between days.
